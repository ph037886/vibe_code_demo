A / B CLASSROOM EXPERIMENT

Expectation & Variance Lab

Equal expected returns can have very different variability.

Model and classroom instructions

In the card-game model, Player A scores with probability 1/3 per valid round, and Player B with probability 2/3. Discarded draws do not enter this lab. Here, A receives +G when A scores and −L when B scores. Every round is independent.

All payments are hypothetical NT$ amounts for teaching. Changing p creates an alternative probability model. A fair payment means zero expected net return. Each experiment uses a fixed number of valid rounds, with no first-to-10 stopping rule.

  1. Compare the Low, Medium and High payment presets.
  2. Keep p = 1/3 and enable the fair-payment lock. Increase L. What stays constant?
  3. Compare the original model with the equal-moments symmetric model.
  4. Run repeated experiments and compare total return with average return.

Same moments, different distributions

Both models have exactly μ = 0 and Var(R) = 20,000 NT$². The symmetric model uses exact ±100√2 internally; the display rounds to two decimals.

The probability of a loss is 2/3 in the original model and 1/2 in the symmetric model. Mean and variance do not determine the entire distribution.

Repeated experiments

One experiment contains n independent valid rounds. Run M experiments to see the distribution of their total or average returns.

Run experiments to compare observed and theoretical values.

Bars show observed frequencies across M experiments. The orange line marks the theoretical mean. Sample variance uses M − 1. The seed reproduces results for unchanged settings.

Total return

E[Sₙ] = nμ
Var(Sₙ) = nσ²

Average return

E[R̄ₙ] = μ
Var(R̄ₙ) = σ²/n

Increasing n changes the distribution of each experiment. Increasing M improves how precisely the simulation estimates that distribution.

Discussion

  • Does zero expected return mean no risk?
  • Can two distributions share a mean and variance but have different loss probabilities?
  • Why can the total become more variable while the average becomes less variable?
Suggested answers

No. The fair Medium plan still has SD = NT$141.42 per valid round. Yes. The original and symmetric models have equal first two moments but different loss probabilities. For independent rounds, Var(Sₙ) = nσ² while Var(R̄ₙ) = σ²/n.
